This course teaches you to **write mathematical proofs**: in words, the way mathematicians have for thousands of years, and in **Lean**, a computer program that checks every step of a proof. It follows the book [*The Mechanics of Proof*](/book/index.html) by Heather Macbeth. The book was written for a university course and is used here with the author's permission for educational purposes.

You need solid Algebra 1 and 2. You don't need calculus: the book never uses it.

## How the pages work

Each lesson mixes two kinds of material, and you can always tell them apart.

**1. The book's own text.** It's marked with a book icon and a gray rule down the left, like the book's Preface at the bottom of this page.

**2. Material added for this course.** Guide notes, algebra refreshers, Lean tips, hints and video explainers always appear in a box like this one:

<CourseNote kind="guide" title="Example of an added note">
Anything in a box labelled **"Added for this course"** was written for this course, not by the book's author. Pages with no book text at all, like [Setup](/setup/) and the [Syllabus](/syllabus/), are written entirely for this course.
</CourseNote>

## How to work through a lesson

- Read the book's text first. The added notes are there for when you get stuck or want to go deeper; skip any you don't need.
- Every gray editor box is **live Lean**. Type in it, and Lean checks your proof as you go. The badge underneath tells you where you stand: *Lean is checking…*, *Lean found an error*, *Not finished yet (sorry)*, or **Proof complete ✓**.
- Your work in each editor is saved in this browser. **Reset** brings back the original exercise.
- Each lesson ends at a **Stopping point** box. That's your signal you're done for the day.

Start with [Set up your computer](/setup/), then go to [Week 1 · Day 1](/week-01/day-1/).
